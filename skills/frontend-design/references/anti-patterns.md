# Design questions

Use these when an interface feels generic or awkward. A familiar pattern is not
itself a defect, and its appearance does not establish who or what produced it.
Name the problem for this product before recommending a different aesthetic.

- **Hierarchy:** Can people distinguish the primary action, supporting context,
  and exceptional state? Changing fonts is not a substitute for ordering content.
- **Structure:** Do cards, tabs, and sections reflect meaningful groups? Repeated
  rows or cards can make comparison easier; variety solely for novelty can hurt it.
- **Density:** Does spacing support scanning and the working context? Neither
  generous whitespace nor compact layouts are universally preferable.
- **Color:** Does color communicate state or hierarchy consistently? Check the
  actual foreground/background combinations, including muted and disabled text.
  A palette's name or mood says nothing about its contrast.
- **Typography:** Is text legible, well prioritized, and appropriate for the
  content? An existing system font or single-family design can be a good fit.
- **Decoration:** Do gradients, borders, shadows, icons, and textures clarify
  something or support the requested character? Remove unnecessary competition
  before adding another effect.
- **Motion:** Does it explain an interaction or delay access to useful content?
  Check interruption and reduced motion when animation is part of the change.
- **Content and states:** Does the layout survive real labels, long values, empty
  results, and errors? Attractive placeholder content can conceal a poor workflow.

Prefer a concrete change tied to the observed problem. Do not replace one stock
look with another merely to avoid a recognizable font, color, or component.
